Livestream Market Gaps

Where a small, automated, fact-checked stream like ours can win viewers. In war and big national elections, channels with over a million subscribers take almost everything. The openings are in slow vote counts in mid-size countries, typhoons in Japanese and Tagalog, eclipses for East Asia, and aurora storms.

Where we can win

Slow or disputed vote counts in mid-size countries

Spanish · Portuguese · Italian
Evidence
Honduras's month-long disputed count drew 1.77M live-stream views, and 68% went to channels under 100k subscribers; one with 6,530 subscribers drew 179k. Italy's justice referendum: 55% to small channels (Il Post, 39k subscribers, drew 163k). Portugal's presidential election: no channel over 1M subscribers streamed it at all.
Contrast
Big national nights are taken: Brazil 86% to channels over 1M, Peru 84%, New York City 80%, Bihar 100%.
Why it fits
Official results feeds update every few minutes and counts run for days, which matches our 20–40 minute refresh. We already voice all three languages.
Next
Spain's snap election on Nov 29 (not measured yet; Spanish TV streams heavily) and Cape Verde on Nov 15 (small). Measuring Spain 2023 and the 2024 US election in Spanish first costs about five searches.
Risk
Medium. YouTube's election-misinformation rules apply: cite the official tally, never call a race, label the AI voice.

Typhoons in Japanese and Tagalog

what we already run
Evidence
Our two Champi streams took 10% of 324k Japanese typhoon live-stream views on a 4,310-subscriber channel named for something else, the best small-channel result in that market. Japanese typhoon markets give 18–22% to small channels. In Tagalog, Champi gave 27% to small channels, and Weather Gazer Philippines (54k subscribers) drew 110k per stream.
Limit
A landfalling super typhoon becomes a broadcaster event: Uwan's Tagalog streams sent 88% of views to channels over 1M.
Next
Late-season Philippine typhoons (November–December) and Sea of Japan coast snow events (December–January).
Risk
Low. The channel's name and branding still say Houston and flight simulation.

Eclipses and meteor showers for Japan and Taiwan

Japanese · Chinese
Evidence
The Mar 3 lunar eclipse drew 2.08M Japanese and 614k Taiwanese live-stream views, with 40% and 44% going to small channels. MED Sky LiVE (19k subscribers) drew 305k; the Taipei Astronomical Museum (12.7k) drew 154k.
Next
The Geminids on Dec 13–14, peaking near 23:00 Japan time on Dec 14 under a thin crescent moon: the best-placed shower of the year for East Asia.
Catch
Viewers come to see the sky. We need our own camera or a licensed feed; our pipeline adds cloud forecasts, timing and where to look.

Geomagnetic storms and aurora alerts

English · Spanish · Italian
Evidence
The November 2025 G4 storm: 231k English live-stream views with 49% to small channels and none to channels over 1M. Spanish and Italian streams behaved the same. It is the only market we measured with no big channel in any language.
Why it fits
NOAA's Space Weather Prediction Center publishes everything as JSON (Kp index, aurora oval, solar wind), and storms are forecast one to three days ahead from solar eruptions. The stream can be fully data-driven.
Catch
Smaller audiences, and the timing depends on the sun.
Risk
Low.

Also promising: US winter storms in Spanish. Winter Storm Fern drew 894k Spanish live-stream views with only 35% going to channels over 1M, though most of the small channels were local Univision and Telemundo stations. The season runs December to February.

Where we can't

  • War, terror and military escalation. Across 29 conflict markets, a median 77% of views went to channels over 1M subscribers and 1% to small ones: the Iran war in English (35.5M views, 89%), Japanese (87%) and Hindi (100%), the Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia (74% in English, 95% in Hindi). This is also where policy and harm risk is highest. The Tagalog safety-tracker idea for Filipino workers in the Gulf found no Tagalog live streams for the Houthi strikes and four for the Iran war; YouTube data cannot tell whether that audience exists, because Philippine news viewing leans on Facebook.
  • Big national election nights. Brazil's first round drew 129M live-stream views, 86% to big channels.
  • Launches and missions. Artemis II drew 47–129M views per language, 82–100% to NASA and broadcasters. JAXA owns its own H3 launches. One exception: Starship in Japanese (44% to small channels, 220k views).
  • Outages and market crashes. People read about them but don't watch them live. The Cloudflare article drew 205k Wikipedia views in one day, while live streams drew 14k.
  • Hindi and Telugu. National networks and district TV channels hold the audience on every topic we measured.

How this was measured

Four research agents listed notable events from October 2025 to October 2026 and the search terms people use for them in each language. For each event and language, the YouTube Data API returned up to 50 completed live streams from the event's window, ranked by views, along with each channel's subscriber count. Streams count only when their title names the event and, for Japanese, Chinese, Hindi, Telugu, Bengali, Thai and Korean, is written in that language's script. Wikipedia page views gave a second, independent measure of interest.

Limits to keep in mind:

  • The 50-stream cap undercounts saturated markets, so their totals are floors.
  • View counts are today's totals, not the audience during the event.
  • "Small" means under 100,000 subscribers. That still includes local TV stations, observatories and newspapers, which carry an established brand.
  • Most markets rest on one or two events.
  • The study sees YouTube only, so audiences that watch news on Facebook are invisible to it.
